LightShow

  • Beat-synced
  • Live audio
  • Windows
  • Free
Desktop softwareWaveLink DMX LightShow

Load a song. Get a light show. Then make it yours.

It listens to the track, then lights it.

Point it at an MP3 and it finds the tempo, the beat grid and the structure — intro, build, drop, breakdown — then builds a full show against them. Every cue lands on a real musical event, and nothing about it is random: the same track always produces the same show.

It lights the way a designer would. Moving heads, pars and the laser take turns, a look holds for a phrase, builds end on a bar of strobe, and every drop lands on a white hit with chases locked to the BPM.

Or play it live.

Switch on Live Audio and it lights whatever the PC is playing — a DJ set in rekordbox, Spotify, a browser. It hears the builds and drops in the mix, follows the live tempo and runs the show by itself, swinging the rig left and right on the beat. A live spectrogram takes the timeline’s place, so you can see what it hears.

Queue a whole set and it moves to the next track on its own — or switch the generator off and build the cues yourself. Every head can be located on its own, aimed, and given the pan and tilt centre your rig actually hangs at.

R20 Generator

  • Avolites Pearl
  • Free
  • In your browser

No personality for a fixture? Photograph its DMX chart or paste the channel list, check each channel, and download the .R20.

For Avolites Pearl / Classic consoles: Pearl 2000, 2004, 2008, 2010, Tiger and Azure. The same file loads into LightShow.

1 Get the channels

📷
Tap to choose or take a photo of the DMX chart page
You can add several photos if the chart spans pages
How to type the channel list

Channels. One per line, starting with its number in the fixture: 1 Dimmer, CH2 Strobe or Channel 3: Red. Number them 1, 2, 3… in the order the chart lists them for the DMX mode you use.

Value ranges. On the lines under a channel, from–to then what it does: 0-7 Open, 8-255 Strobe slow-fast. Leading zeros are fine (000-007), and to or ~ work instead of the dash. A plain 0–255 channel needs no ranges.

Percentages. A range whose text says slow-fast, dim-bright or 0-100% shows on the console as a percentage across that range.

16-bit channels. Call the second one “Fine”: 6 Pan Fine pairs itself with Pan.

Names choose the wheel. Dimmer, Strobe, Red, Green, Blue, White, Amber, UV, Pan, Tilt, Colour, Gobo, Prism, Zoom, Focus, Speed, Auto, Reset. You can change any channel's function in step 3.

Length. The console shows 12 characters of a name and 11 of a range; longer text is shortened for you.

DMX address. Don't type it here. The file lists the fixture's own channels 1, 2, 3…; the start address is set on the fixture and chosen when you patch it on the Pearl.

    Loading it on the console
    
    
      
    Pearl 2008 / 2010 / Tiger read personalities from a USB stick in this layout (the zip already has it):
    
      
    AVOLITES\FIXTURES\R20\<MANUFACTURER>\<FILE>.R20
    
      
    The console also expects the shape file SG.DAT in the R20 folder. Copy it from any Avolites personality download or use Avolites' Copy 2008 tool. Older floppy-disk Pearls take the .R20 in the root of the disk with SG.DAT.
    
      
    Then patch: Patch → Fixtures → (disk) → choose manufacturer → fixture. Patch one fixture and check each wheel before a show.
    
      
    Macros (lamp on / reset) aren't generated; add them in a text editor if you need them.
